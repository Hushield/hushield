# Code of Conduct

## Our pledge

We want HuShield to be a project where people can contribute without being harassed, belittled, or
driven off. Everyone participating — contributors, reviewers, issue reporters, maintainers — is
expected to help keep it that way.

We pledge to make participation a harassment-free experience for everyone, regardless of age, body
size, visible or invisible disability, ethnicity, sex characteristics, gender identity and
expression, level of experience, education, socio-economic status, nationality, personal appearance,
race, religion, or sexual identity and orientation.

## Expected behavior

- Be direct about code and kind about people. Rigorous technical criticism is welcome and wanted;
  contempt for the person who wrote the code is not.
- Assume competence and good faith. Someone unfamiliar with this codebase is not stupid — our docs
  are probably just incomplete.
- Accept that reviewers may decline your change, and that authors may push back on your review.
  Disagreement is normal; disrespect is not.
- Remember that "obvious" is relative. A question that seems basic to you may be the reason someone
  else stays or leaves.

## Unacceptable behavior

- Harassment, intimidation, or discrimination in any form
- Sexualized language or imagery, or unwelcome sexual attention
- Personal or political attacks, insults, or derogatory comments
- Publishing others' private information without explicit permission
- Sustained disruption of discussions, or deliberately derailing issues and PRs
- Dismissing or demeaning contributors for their level of experience

## Scope

This applies in all project spaces — issues, pull requests, commit messages, code review, and
discussions — and whenever someone is representing the project in public.

## Reporting

Report concerns to **john@brahy.com**. Reports are handled confidentially. You will get an
acknowledgement, and you will not be pressured to confront the other person directly.

If your report concerns a maintainer, it still goes to the same address, and it will still be taken
seriously.

## Enforcement

Maintainers are responsible for clarifying and enforcing these standards, and may take any action
they judge appropriate — from a private warning, to editing or removing comments and commits, to a
temporary or permanent ban from the project.

Maintainers who do not follow or enforce this Code of Conduct in good faith may face consequences
determined by the project's other maintainers.
