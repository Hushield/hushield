# HuShield Trademark Policy

The HuShield **code** is licensed under [Apache-2.0](LICENSE). The HuShield **name and brand** are
not. This document explains the difference, because Apache-2.0 grants copyright and patent rights but
deliberately does not grant trademark rights (see section 6 of the license).

## What the license already gives you

You may use, modify, distribute, and sell the code — commercially or otherwise, publicly or
privately — without asking permission. You may run a modified version as a service. You may fork the
project permanently. None of that requires our approval, and this policy does not restrict it.

- The names **HuShield** and **hushield.com**
- The HuShield logo, wordmark, and visual identity
- The HuShield App Store listing identity, including the bundle identifier `com.brahy.hushield` and

## What this means in practice

**You must rename a fork you distribute.** If you publish a modified build — to the App Store, to
TestFlight, to a package registry, or as a hosted service — give it your own name. Do not call it
HuShield, do not call it "HuShield Plus" or "HuShield Community Edition", and do not use the logo.
This is the one rule that matters most: users must be able to tell whose software they are trusting
with their call data.

**You must not imply endorsement.** Do not suggest your fork, product, or service is official,
approved, affiliated with, or maintained by the HuShield project when it is not.

**You must not register confusing marks or domains.** Do not register trademarks, domain names, app
names, or social accounts that are confusingly similar to HuShield.

## What you may always do without asking

- **Refer to the project by name**, accurately and descriptively. "Built on HuShield", "compatible
  with the HuShield API", "a fork of HuShield", "imports HuShield's blocklist format" are all fine.
- **Say what your fork is derived from.** In fact, please do — attribution is welcome.
- Use the name in articles, talks, tutorials, comparisons, and reviews.
- Use the name in the unmodified upstream package, repo, or module path when redistributing HuShield
  itself unchanged.

The line is straightforward: use the name to **describe** the software truthfully, not to **brand**
something as ours that we don't maintain.

## Why this exists

HuShield's entire value claim is that it holds no personal data. A rebranded fork that quietly
collects phone numbers or contacts, shipping under our name, would damage the exact trust the project
is built on — and users would have no way to tell the difference. Reserving the name is how the
project stays accountable for what carries it, while leaving the code as open as possible.
